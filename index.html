Three Targets That Pay Immediately

  1. Biochemistry = Vitamins. 10 of 25 Biochem questions in 2022–23 were vitamins. One weekend on deficiencies, coenzyme roles and toxicities is the densest single target in the paper.
  2. Pharmacology = CVS drugs first. 8 of 27, and Pharmacology has the shortest stems of any subject — pure one-liner recall, perfect for duty fragments.
  3. ENT = Pharynx + Ear. 11 of 15 questions sit in those two chapters. A subject you can nearly close in two sittings.

How these numbers were built

Weightage is measured only from real exam papers. Recency-weighted 2018 ×0.6 → 2023 ×1.6, because the exam moved to its current pattern in 2021.

Load (1 = light, 5 = heavy) blends three things: how big the syllabus is, how many topics you must cover to reach 80% of that subject's marks, and how long its question stems run (short stem = one-liner recall; long stem = multi-step vignette). Yield = marks ÷ load, rescaled so the best subject is 100.

Honest limits. The syllabus-size part of Load is Dr. Andy's editorial estimate, not a measurement — nothing in a question bank tells you how big a syllabus is. Topic counts come from the 400 questions of 2022–23 that carry official topic tags, so treat the ordering as the signal and small differences as noise. 11% of questions could not be assigned a subject and were dropped rather than guessed. Question banks branded 2012–2016 were excluded: they are one recycled pool, and NEET PG did not exist before 2017.

Order of Attack

  1. Bank the cheap marks first: OBGYN, Biochemistry (vitamins), Microbiology, PSM, Pharmacology (CVS) — the best marks per hour on the paper.
  2. Then the heavyweights: Surgery and Medicine — 146 marks between them, but expensive. Vignette + image drills.
  3. Short subjects in duty fragments; images every single week.
  4. Leave late: Dermatology, Radiology, Anaesthesia, Psychiatry — 46 marks combined.

This order comes from 5 real papers — see the Yield tab for the numbers behind it.
